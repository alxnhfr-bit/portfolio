01Live · Flagship

SundayAtlas

AI travel planning, powered by the creators you trust.

Try SundayAtlas ↗
What the video shows
  1. A trip, planned in one conversation.
  2. Describe the trip in your own words.
  3. Pick dates, or leave them loose.
  4. Tap a few moods. It reads between the lines.
  5. The concierge drafts every day and checks every address.
  6. Your week, as a guide-book chapter.
  7. Days are pages. Tap to turn.
  8. Notes stick to stops.
  9. Navigation hands off to Google Maps.
  10. And it’s on your shelf, ready for September.

The Problem

Planning a trip means juggling 5 to 10 different platforms.

Itineraries in one tab. Hotels in another. Activities somewhere else. Bookings in a fourth. The experience is fragmented, repetitive, and exhausting.

The Insight

The best travel recommendations don’t come from algorithms; they come from creators you already trust.

An AI concierge that takes the YouTube videos you’d watch anyway and turns them into draft itineraries, with every place verified, sourced, and one click away from being in your trip.

The Product

SundayAtlas is a live travel product, not a closed prototype.

An AI concierge that builds day-by-day itineraries from a natural-language prompt. Every place is verified live against Google Places; every recommendation is traceable to a source. The differentiator: it extracts named spots from the YouTube creators you already follow, so a video you’d watch anyway becomes a draft itinerary in one click. Plus bookings, share links, an installable PWA, and an affiliate-tracking rail under the hood.

The Takeaway

Trust is the product, not the itinerary.

Users don’t abandon trip planners because the suggestions are bad. They abandon them because they don’t trust a single tool to replace their own research. The real design challenge is earning that trust incrementally.

Planning

  • AI concierge builds day-by-day itineraries from a natural-language prompt.
  • Every place verified live against Google Places, with provenance badges (Eater, Michelin, ratings).
  • Trip essentials (visa, plugs, SIM, transit) and travel preferences carry across every trip.

Discovery: the differentiator

  • Trending YouTube content per destination, grouped into Eats / Sip / Do / Stay.
  • AI extracts named places from any video → one-click add to your trip with creator credit.
  • Paste your own YouTube link; SundayAtlas pulls its places.

Bookings & polish

  • Bookings as first-class items: check-in/out, nights, confirmation, “Booked” badge.
  • Booking.com search (dates pre-filled) routed through a click-tracking + affiliate rail.
  • Read-only share links, installable PWA, consent banner, self-service data deletion.

Under the hood

  • Server-side agent loop, up to 5 iterations per turn, with web search as agentic tool use.
  • Production trade-offs made explicitly: prompt design, context management across sessions, model choice weighed against cost and latency.
  • Guardrails and explicit fallback states, added after a silent fallback shipped output that was plausible and wrong.

02Live · App Store

15GRMS

A coffee brewing companion for iPhone.

Download on the App Store ↗

The Problem

Brewing by hand means watching a clock and a scale at the same time.

Every step has its own water target and its own timing. Generic timers do not know the recipe, and written recipes do not keep time, so you end up reading one while counting against the other, with a kettle in your other hand.

The Insight

Run the recipe as the timer, not alongside it.

Each step shows how much water should be on the scale at that moment and counts down against the wall clock, so you can watch the kettle instead of the phone.

The Product

Shipped on the App Store: twelve recipes across three methods, free, and it makes no network requests at all.

React and TypeScript in a Capacitor shell, iOS 16 and up, 2.6 MB. Your last brew waits on the home screen with the dose you used, so Start to pouring takes about five seconds. Change dose, ratio or temperature and every pour target recalculates live, remembered per recipe. No account, no ads, no tracking; recipes and fonts are bundled, which is why the App Store privacy answer is “Data Not Collected” and why that claim is verifiable rather than asserted.

The Takeaway

The hard part is the part that has to keep working while the screen is off.

A phone gets locked mid-brew, which suspends the web view entirely, so a counter-based timer would drift or stall. Steps run on absolute end timestamps instead, and when several fall overdue at once each one chains from the previous step’s scheduled end rather than from the current time, landing on the same schedule the notifications were already set against. That single decision is the difference between a demo and a timer you can trust with a kettle in your hand.

Brewing

  • Twelve recipes across V60, AeroPress and cold brew, each step showing its cumulative pour target.
  • Your last brew waits on the home screen with the dose you used; Start to pouring is about five seconds.
  • Dose, ratio and temperature adjust live, and the change is remembered per recipe.

The timer

  • Steps run on absolute end timestamps, so the count stays correct through a locked screen.
  • Overdue steps chain from the previous step’s scheduled end, not from the current time.
  • Notifications are only requested for the two multi-hour steeps, since iOS grants one prompt per install.

The journal

  • Every brew is logged the moment it finishes, with nothing to type.
  • Rate it, tag how it tasted, add notes if you want to. Swipe to delete.
  • Export the whole journal as JSON through the iOS share sheet.

Coaching and craft

  • Too bitter, sour or thin? One tap suggests a single conservative adjustment and explains why.
  • The adjustment is applied the next time you brew that recipe.
  • Three palettes in light and dark; brew methods are coded by shape, never by colour.

Signal

Autonomous market-signal agent, with the eval harness to prove it works.

Eval loopNode.js · GitHub Actions · Sheets · Claude Haiku
1 · TaxonomyFive closed labels, explicit precedence rules for ties
2 · RubricOne document: human labeling guide and classifier prompt spec
3 · Golden setBlind-labeled before seeing any prediction
4 · HarnessDeterministic Python scoring, no LLM judge
5 · ScorePer-bucket precision, recall, F1, confusion matrix
6 · Fix, re-score ↺Recent posts instead of all-time top: one bucket went from 63% of the sample to 33%

The Problem

An agent that is confidently wrong is worse than no agent.

I built an agent to mine travel subreddits for product signal, and it looked like it worked. Every post came back neatly sorted into one of four buckets. But there was no bucket for posts carrying no signal at all, so the agent was forced to file noise as insight, and it did so with total confidence.

The Insight

You cannot improve a classifier you have not measured.

Bucket counts tell you nothing about whether the buckets are right. The only way to know is to grade the agent against answers you labeled yourself, before you saw what it predicted.

The Build

A closed loop: taxonomy, rubric, golden set, harness, fix, re-score.

A Node.js agent running on GitHub Actions, logging to Sheets, classifying posts with Claude Haiku. On top of it: a five-label closed taxonomy with explicit precedence rules for ties, a written rubric that doubles as both the human labeling guide and the classifier prompt spec, a blind-labeled golden set, and a deterministic Python scoring harness (no LLM judge) that outputs per-bucket precision, recall, F1, and a confusion matrix. Fixing data collection mattered as much as fixing the model: the agent had been pulling all-time-top posts instead of recent ones, and correcting that moved one bucket from 63 percent of the sample down to 33 percent.

The Takeaway

Shipping an agent is the easy part. Knowing whether it is right is the job.

The failure was not the model, it was the taxonomy. No amount of prompt tuning fixes a scheme that has nowhere to put the truth. The eval did not just score the agent, it told me what to change.

Build 1 · n8n

The first build.

Build 2 · Zapier, what the platform costs
  • No parallel branching
  • No structured array passing between steps
  • Response-size cap on webhooks
  • One email per match instead of an aggregated digest

The Problem

Twenty careers pages, checked by hand, every morning.

The Insight

The interesting question was never whether to automate it. It was what each automation platform quietly takes away from you.

The Build

Multi-stage orchestration across 20+ APIs.

Source collection, pre-filter on title, level, and location, then Claude as the scoring layer against a written rubric, deduplication, and a ranked daily digest. Built first on n8n, then rebuilt end to end on Zapier to document the trade-offs: no parallel branching, no structured array passing between steps, a response-size cap on webhooks, and one email per match instead of an aggregated digest.

The Takeaway

The hard part was not the plumbing, it was encoding the rubric.

An agent that scores things is only as good as the definition of “good” you can actually write down.

05Prototype

Rise

AI Health & Fitness OS

View the prototype ↗

The Problem

Your health data is everywhere. And nowhere.

Sleep, nutrition, training load, stress: the signals that matter most are scattered across disconnected apps. Despite being deeply correlated, they never talk to each other.

The Insight

One experience. Every signal. AI that understands the connections.

Unifying workout tracking, macro logging, wearable data, and meditation with an AI coach that understands how these systems interact could fundamentally change how people manage their health.

The Prototype

A complete consumer health app, designed and prototyped end to end.

Built with React and TypeScript. Integrates workout tracking, nutrition logging, wearable health data via third-party APIs, guided meditation, and AI-powered coaching in a single unified experience.

The Takeaway

The hardest design problem wasn’t adding features; it was deciding what not to surface.

When every health signal is available, the AI coach becomes the editorial layer. The biggest unlock was shifting from “show all the data” to “show the one insight that matters today.”
